Enterprise Data Has Changed
Today's enterprise information is no longer stored in one place.
It is distributed across Windows file servers, NAS, SharePoint, cloud storage, FTP servers and employee devices. At the same time, employees, customers, partners and contractors all need secure access to that information.
Organizations have invested heavily in identity security, endpoint protection and Zero Trust Network Access. Yet the data people actually need often remains governed by legacy permissions and disconnected sharing tools.
The challenge is no longer where data is stored.
The challenge is how access to that data is governed.

The Access-to-Data Gap
Identity platforms verify who users are.
ZTNA determines whether they can reach a network or application.
But neither necessarily governs which individual files and folders users should access after they are authenticated.
As a result, organizations often grant broader access than necessary or copy information into separate collaboration platforms.
Zero Trust should extend beyond the connection and all the way to the data.
[Graphic — The Zero Trust Data-Layer Gap]
________________________________________
Moving Data Isn't the Answer
Many collaboration platforms require organizations to upload, synchronize or duplicate information into another repository before it can be shared.
That creates additional copies of sensitive information, fragmented permissions, version confusion and greater administrative complexity.
A better approach is to leave information where it already resides and govern access directly.
Move governance—not data.
________________________________________
Why It Matters
When users receive access to more information than they need, the impact of compromised credentials, insider threats and ransomware can increase dramatically.
Applying least-privilege access at the file and folder level helps reduce unnecessary exposure while enabling secure collaboration across existing enterprise storage.
The goal is simple:
Give users access only to the information they need—and nothing more.
